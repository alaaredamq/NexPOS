The idea: a locked box

Today the shop sends its data to the cloud as plain, readable data. With sealing, the shop PC locks it first. The cloud only ever holds locked boxes.

Shop PC

Makes the key. Locks the data before it leaves the shop. Keeps selling exactly as now.

9f3a07… locked data

Cloud

Stores the locked boxes. It does not have the key, so it cannot open them.

9f3a07… still locked

Owner's phone

Holds the key. Opens the data on the phone, and only there.

The key never goes through the cloud. It goes from the shop PC to the phone by scanning a QR code on the PC screen, like linking WhatsApp Web.

What if a key is lost?

Owners lose things, so the design assumes it will happen. Most losses cost nothing. Only losing everything at once is a real problem.

What was lostWhat happensCost
The phoneScan the QR code again from the shop PC.Nothing
The phone and the slip, the shop PC is fineThe PC makes a new key and uploads everything again. The old cloud copy is discarded.Nothing
The shop PC, the phone is linkedThe phone still reads the cloud copy. The new PC gets the key from the phone's QR code or from the slip, and restores the shop's own backup.Nothing
The PC and the phone, the slip is keptThe slip opens everything on the new PC.A bit of time

Assisted recovery: the owner chooses

One tick at set-up. It can be switched off later. It is never on by default.

TickedA copy of the key, sealed so that only the provider's offline key can open it, is stored in the cloud. The provider can help after a disaster, and writes it in the client's history.
Not tickedNothing is stored. Nobody can recover the key for the owner. Fully private, fully the owner's responsibility.
NoteThe "only the provider's offline key" part is technical. The "checks who is asking" part is a procedure, not a lock. A short code such as the 4-digit PIN is never used to protect a key, because it can be guessed in seconds.

Who sees what

Everything is sealed unless it is on a short, explicit list of things the cloud needs. A new table added next year is private by default.

Sealed

The cloud cannot read it. Only the owner's phone can.

  • Customers, balances and debt history
  • Orders, receipts, receipt lines, payments
  • Cash drawer sessions and drops
  • Purchases and suppliers
  • Staff accounts (names, roles)
  • Stock history, the shop's address and tax number
  • Product cost price

Readable

On purpose, so the owner can change prices from the phone later.

  • Products, categories, selling prices
  • Taxes, discounts, payment methods
  • Whether the shop is connected, the 24-hour strip
  • Subscription dates
Unavoidable metadata: table names, row numbers, how many rows, and when changes arrive and how big they are. That is why the portal can still show "435 orders" without reading a single order. Staff passwords and PINs never leave the shop at all.

Honest limits

Sealing protects privacy. It is not magic. These are worth telling clients plainly.

DeletingSomeone with server access could delete or roll back locked rows. They cannot read or change them (a changed row fails to open), but missing rows can go unnoticed unless the phone app compares counts with the shop.
The keyIt is only as safe as the places it is kept: the shop PC, the owner's phone and the slip. A stolen unlocked phone with the app open can read the data.
A web pageA page served from the provider's own server is weaker than an installed phone app: the server could change the page to capture the key. That is why the phone app comes first.
Already uploadedAnything the test shops uploaded before sealing stays readable in old disk copies and server backups. Seal before real clients connect.

The plan

Each step ends with something that works and is tested on its own.

  1. Lock and store Built

    Key creation and protection on the shop PC, the sealed and readable lists, locked rows in the sync, and a place for them in the cloud. Portal counts still work. If the key can't be opened, the shop uploads nothing rather than sending readable data.

  2. Shop PC screens Built

    Settings, Online, Privacy: the recovery code behind the PIN, printed or saved as a PDF, and the "I kept it" confirmation. Nothing uploads until it is confirmed, and the whole shop is then sent again, locked. A PC that lost its key can enter the code from the slip. The portal shows "Sealed" per branch. The assisted-recovery tick waits for step 4, and the QR code on the slip for step 5.

  3. Owner access and the phone app

    Owners get their own sign-in and a read-only way to fetch locked rows. The phone app opens them with the key.

  4. Assisted recovery

    The provider's offline key pair, the offline recovery tool, and the entry in the client's history.

  5. Link my phone on the PC

    PIN, QR code and audit entry. Can be built together with step 3.

Steps 1 and 2 (highlighted) already stop the provider reading client data, even before the phone app exists.

Decisions taken (say if you want any changed)

  1. Product cost price sealed. It shows the shop's margin. Selling prices stay readable.
  2. Staff accounts sealed. Together with roles and permissions.
  3. Every new connection sealed, no opt-out. Applies from step 2, when the screens exist.
  4. Phone app only, no browser viewer. A page from the provider's server is the weaker option.
  5. Shop settings and stock history sealed. Checked: they hold the address, tax number, stock movements and staff names.
